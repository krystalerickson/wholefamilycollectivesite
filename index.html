Why Caregivers?

A child may spend an hour with a specialist. Their caregiver is there for the other 23.

For many parents, discovering that their child may have a developmental delay or disability can be one of life’s biggest shocks. Stigma and misunderstanding can leave families feeling isolated, while specialized services are often limited, costly, or difficult to access. On top of that, caregivers are continuously navigating the pressures of family, finances, relationships, and their own emotional and physical wellbeing—while simply trying to keep their families afloat.

They can be left carrying enormous responsibility with little practical support—wondering: What does my child need? How can I help? Where do I go? What happens next?

Caregivers show up for their children in a thousand small ways every day.

52.9M children under 5 are estimated to have a developmental disability globally. Olusanya et al., Lancet Global Health, 2018 (Global Burden of Disease Study 2016)
95% of those children—an estimated 50.2M—live in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Olusanya et al., Lancet Global Health, 2018
16.3% of children ages 0–3 in a 2025 Nairobi baseline study were developmentally "off-track"—3× the rate of an international peer sample. Aga Khan University Institute for Human Development, "A Baseline Study on the State of Early Childhood Development in Nairobi," 2025 (n=2,903 households)

But numbers only tell half the story.

They don't capture the caregiver trying to understand why their child isn't speaking. Or wondering whether they're doing enough. Or feeling exhausted after constantly advocating, coordinating, teaching, and caring for their child—often while simply trying to keep their family afloat.

That's why we start with caregivers. They are among the most important influences in a child's everyday environment—creating opportunities for communication, learning, and connection in ordinary moments: playing, eating, getting dressed, simply being together.

A child's development doesn't happen only in clinics or classrooms. It happens in the thousands of ordinary moments families share every day.

We strengthen caregivers':

Knowledge

Understanding their child's development, strengths, and needs.

Confidence

Feeling capable of supporting their child and recognizing opportunities to help.

Skills

Using practical strategies during everyday routines and activities.

Agency

Advocating for their child and making informed decisions about their family's needs.

Wellbeing

Reducing isolation and helping caregivers feel more supported and able to sustain their role.

Connection

Building relationships with other caregivers, practitioners, organizations, and community resources.

When caregivers benefit, children benefit too. Both deserve more opportunities to flourish in the environments where they spend their lives.

What We Do

Bringing evidence-based support closer to families.

Illustrative image

Our first intervention is WHO Caregiver Skills Training (CST), an evidence-based program designed to equip caregivers with practical strategies for communication, engagement, daily living skills, challenging behaviors, and caregiver wellbeing.

CST uses everyday activities as opportunities for learning and is designed to be delivered by trained non-specialists, making it particularly relevant where access to specialized services is limited.

Our job isn't simply to deliver a program. We want to understand what works, what it costs, what needs to change, and what it takes to make effective caregiver support sustainable at scale.

How We Work

With our heads. With our hearts. With our whole selves.

Head:
Evidence

We follow the evidence.

We use approaches grounded in rigorous research and measure what happens in practice. We test assumptions, learn from implementation, and change course when the evidence changes.

Heart:
Family

We build with, not for.

Caregivers are experts in their own families. Practitioners, community organizations, and local leaders bring knowledge and relationships that cannot be replicated from the outside. We listen first and design alongside the people closest to the problem.

Soul:
Community

We strengthen what already exists.

We won't try to do everything ourselves. We work through trusted community networks, build local capacity, and partner where others are better positioned. Over time, we want effective approaches to become part of sustainable systems rather than parallel ones.

Where We Work

Starting in Nairobi. Building across Kenya.

Why Kenya?

Kenya is at an important moment for disability inclusion and early childhood development. A new Persons with Disabilities National Policy was adopted in 2024, followed by a new Persons with Disabilities Act in 2025 and a Joint Disability Inclusion Strategy for 2025–2027.

The policy direction is increasingly clear. The opportunity now is implementation.

Kenya also has a growing ecosystem of organizations, practitioners, researchers, advocates, and government partners already working across developmental disabilities, early childhood development, health, parenting, and social services.

We don't want to reinvent that ecosystem. We want to learn from it, contribute to it, and help make it stronger.

Why Nairobi?

We're starting in Nairobi because of its combination of need, infrastructure, and opportunity. Nairobi has a strong concentration of organizations, health and education institutions, researchers, practitioners, community organizations, and government stakeholders working across the issues that matter to families.

It gives us the opportunity to work closely with families and local partners, test what works in a real-world setting, and build the relationships and infrastructure needed for sustainable scale.

Nairobi is our starting point, not our endpoint. Our goal is to build an approach that can be sustainably delivered across Kenya—and to share what we learn with others working to expand evidence-based caregiver support around the world.

Nairobi is where we start. Kenya is where we aim to grow.
