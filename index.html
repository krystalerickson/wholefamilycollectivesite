Why Caregivers?

A child spends an hour with a specialist. Their caregiver is there for the other 23.

Discovering a child has a developmental delay or disability is overwhelming. In Kenya, stigma isolates families, and specialized support is rare and unaffordable. Caregivers often carry this alone.

291M children and adolescents (ages 0–19) worldwide are affected by a developmental disability. Olusanya et al., 2020
10× A child is more likely to have a developmental disability by age 5 than to die before it. Olusanya et al., Lancet Global Health, 2021
16.3% of Nairobi toddlers are developmentally off-track—3× the international rate. Aga Khan University, 2025

A child’s development doesn’t only happen in clinics. It happens at home—playing, eating, getting dressed. We turn those everyday moments into opportunities for growth.

Our Work

Equipping caregivers. Strengthening systems.

Launching soon

Caregiver Skills Training

We’re starting with the WHO Caregiver Skills Training program in Nairobi. Field-tested in 30+ countries, it empowers caregivers with communication, daily living, and wellbeing strategies through daily routines.

Coming next

Peer Support

We want caregivers to build meaningful support networks. Once our first cohort completes training, we’ll begin building peer networks where caregivers can share experiences and learn from one another.

Long-term

Ecosystem Strengthening

Over time, we aim to work with government and system partners to scale effective caregiver support nationally.

Where We Work

Starting in Nairobi. Building across Kenya.

Kenya has made meaningful progress on disability inclusion, with a new National Policy in 2024, Disability Act in 2025, and national strategy for 2025–2027.

The foundation is set. The work now is implementation.

We start in Nairobi, where the need is high and the local ecosystem is strong. We aim to test what works and build toward national scale.
