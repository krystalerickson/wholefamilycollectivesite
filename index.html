Whole Family Collective

Stronger caregivers. Stronger families. Stronger futures.

We help caregivers help themselves—alongside their children.

Whole Family Collective strengthens the people at the center of a child's life. We equip caregivers with practical, evidence-based skills that support their own confidence and wellbeing, while creating opportunities for their children to communicate, learn, connect, and grow.

For many parents, discovering that their child may have a developmental delay or disability can be one of life’s biggest shocks. Stigma and misunderstanding can leave families feeling isolated, while specialized services are often limited, costly, or difficult to access. On top of that, caregivers are continuously navigating the pressures of family, finances, relationships, and their own emotional and physical wellbeing, while simply trying to keep their families afloat.

They can be left carrying enormous responsibility with little practical support—leading to stress, loneliness, and uncertainty about the future. Many are left wondering: What does my child need? How can I help? Where do I go? What happens next?

We're starting in Nairobi, with the goal of building for families across Kenya.

Why Caregivers

A child may spend an hour with a specialist. Their caregiver is there for the other 23.

Caregivers show up for their children in a thousand small ways every day—often while navigating stigma, limited services, and their own exhaustion. We start with them because that's where a child's development actually happens: in ordinary moments, not specialist appointments.

52.9M children under 5 are estimated to have a developmental disability globally. Olusanya et al., Lancet Global Health, 2018 (Global Burden of Disease Study 2016)
95% of those children—an estimated 50.2M—live in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Olusanya et al., Lancet Global Health, 2018
16.3% of children ages 0–3 in a 2025 Nairobi baseline study were developmentally "off-track"—3x the rate of an international peer sample. Aga Khan University Institute for Human Development, "A Baseline Study on the State of Early Childhood Development in Nairobi," 2025 (n=2,903 households)

But numbers only tell half the story.

They don't capture the caregiver trying to understand why their child isn't speaking. Or wondering whether they are doing enough. Or feeling exhausted after constantly advocating, coordinating, teaching, and caring for their child, often with limited support.

That's why we start with caregivers.

Caregivers are one of the most important influences in a child's everyday environment. They create thousands of opportunities for communication, learning, connection, and participation in ordinary moments—while playing, eating, getting dressed, or simply spending time together.

As essential partners in their child's development, caregivers are often overlooked. They need support, too.

We strengthen caregivers':

Knowledge

Understanding their child's development, strengths, and needs.

Confidence

Feeling capable of supporting their child and recognizing opportunities to help.

Skills

Using practical strategies during everyday routines and activities.

Agency

Advocating for their child and making informed decisions about their family's needs.

Wellbeing

Reducing isolation and helping caregivers feel more supported and able to sustain their role.

Connection

Building relationships with other caregivers, practitioners, organizations, and community resources.

When caregivers benefit, children benefit too. Both deserve more opportunities to flourish in the environments where they spend their lives.

What We Do

We do everything with our heads, hearts, and souls.

Head:
Evidence-Based

We use approaches grounded in rigorous research and measure what happens in practice. Our first intervention is the WHO Caregiver Skills Training (CST) program, an evidence-based intervention tested in 30+ countries, designed to equip caregivers with practical strategies for communication, engagement, daily living skills, challenging behaviors, and caregiver wellbeing.

CST uses everyday activities as opportunities for learning and is designed to be delivered by trained non-specialists, making it relevant where access to specialized services is limited. We're working with local partners to implement, evaluate, and strengthen the approach in real-world settings.

Our goal isn't simply to deliver a program, but to understand what works, what it costs, what needs to change, and how effective support can reach more families. We're not afraid to try new approaches if the evidence changes, but we stay committed to our families through it all.

Heart:
Family-Centered

We build with—not for—caregivers, practitioners, and local organizations, drawing on the expertise already present in communities. Caregivers are the experts on their own children; nobody understands a child more intuitively than they do. We seek their input at every stage—before program design, throughout implementation, and after, with dedicated periods for reflection.

We aim to bring compassion and dignity to every interaction, and to co-design and incorporate their feedback. We're less interested in lofty theoretical ideas than in practical support used during the everyday routines and relationships families already have.

Soul:
Community-Rooted

We believe in long-term commitment and sustainability. Our approach is holistic—supporting the entire family unit, from primary caregivers to siblings and extended family—to build strong family and community bonds. We're starting with caregiver skills training, but our long-term goal is to add programs and refer families to the right resources to support their long-term growth.

We aim to work alongside both public and private sector partners to strengthen existing infrastructure rather than create parallel systems, and to build toward long-term sustainability from day one.

Where We Work

We're starting in Nairobi and building across Kenya.

Why Kenya?

Kenya is at an important moment for disability inclusion and early childhood development. The country adopted a Persons with Disabilities National Policy in 2024, establishing a national framework for advancing the rights and inclusion of people with disabilities. In 2025, Kenya passed a new Persons with Disabilities Act, strengthening the institutional framework for disability rights and explicitly addressing children's rights, early identification, rehabilitation, inclusive education, and coordination between national and county governments.

Kenya has also launched a Joint Disability Inclusion Strategy for 2025–2027, bringing together government, civil society, private-sector, development, and UN partners around coordinated action.

Thanks to the efforts of dozens of organizations already working in this space, disability advocacy and inclusion are becoming an increasing national priority. There's still a long way to go, but the policy direction is increasingly clear. The opportunity now is implementation.

We don't want to reinvent Kenya's existing ecosystem of organizations, practitioners, researchers, and government partners. We want to learn from it, contribute to it, and help make it stronger.

Why Nairobi?

We're starting in Nairobi because of its combination of need, infrastructure, and opportunity. Nairobi has a strong concentration of health and education institutions, researchers, practitioners, community organizations, and government stakeholders working across the issues that matter to families.

It gives us the opportunity to work closely with families and local partners, test what works, and build the relationships and infrastructure needed for sustainable scale.

We believe a rising tide lifts all boats. Long-term, we hope to expand nationwide and support other WHO CST implementers globally.

Our Promise

Our goal isn't simply to deliver a program, but to help families feel more capable, connected, and hopeful about their future.

We're committed to being honest with every stakeholder we serve:

Caregivers

We exist to build their confidence, skills, self-efficacy, agency, and support networks, so they can support their child's development, strengthen their family's wellbeing, and thrive together over the long term.

Children

We exist to improve their developmental outcomes by strengthening the capacity of their caregivers and connecting families to broader networks of developmental support when needed.

Partners

We exist to strengthen the networks that already support families. We expect to begin by working with trusted community and faith-based organizations, building local capacity and learning what works in practice. Over time, we aim to deepen partnerships with government and other public systems, so effective approaches can be sustainably supported by the systems that serve families at scale.

Our Team

We exist to create an environment where mission-driven people can grow, lead, and do the highest-impact work of their careers, while being supported to do it well and sustainably.

Funders

We exist to provide a transparent, rigorously evaluated investment that generates practical implementation insights, honest reporting, and evidence to strengthen the broader developmental disability ecosystem.

Board & Advisors

We exist to equip them with clear evidence, implementation insights, and ecosystem context, so they can provide exceptional strategic guidance, open doors we couldn't open alone, and advance the field.

Families can't flourish alone. Neither can we.
