Why Caregivers?

Caregivers often carry this alone.

Discovering a child has a developmental delay or disability is overwhelming. In Kenya, stigma isolates families, and specialized support is rare and unaffordable. Caregivers often carry this alone.

Where We Work & Why Now

Starting in Nairobi. Building across Kenya.

Kenya has made meaningful progress on disability inclusion, with three major recent milestones: Persons with Disabilities National Policy (2024), Persons with Disabilities Act (2025), and the Joint Disability Inclusion Strategy (2025–2027).

Together, these mark a shift from a charity-based approach to a coordinated, rights-based framework for disability inclusion. For caregivers and their children with developmental delays and disabilities, this means stronger legal protections, a mandate for early identification and intervention, and coordinated action across health, education, and social protection systems.

The foundation is set. The work now is implementation.

We start in Nairobi, where the need is high and the local ecosystem is strong. We aim to test what works and build toward national scale.

Our Theory of Change

Equip caregivers. Learn what works.
Build what lasts.

Caregivers play a central role in supporting children’s development. Evidence shows that caregiver-mediated interventions can strengthen their own wellbeing, skills, and self-confidence and help support children’s communication, engagement, daily living skills, and positive behavior.

We have an evidence-based starting point. In partnership with existing efforts, we hope to answer:

  • Does it help caregivers?
  • Can we deliver it well in Nairobi?
  • What does it cost?
  • How can new evidence and technology help us do it better?
  • What does it take to sustain and scale?

We will share what we learn openly.
