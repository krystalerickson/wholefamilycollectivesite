Why Caregivers?

A child may spend an hour with a specialist. Their caregiver is there for the other 23.

Discovering a developmental delay or disability can be overwhelming. In Kenya, stigma leaves families isolated, while specialized care remains scarce and expensive. Caregivers often shoulder this burden alone—balancing emotional strain, daily survival, and a system with few clear answers.

They are left carrying enormous responsibility with little practical support, asking:

  • What does my child need?
  • How can I help?
  • Where do I turn next?

Caregivers show up in a thousand small ways every day. We provide the tools, support, and community they need to turn those ordinary moments into progress.

The Scale of Need

52.9M children under 5 globally estimated to have a developmental disability. Lancet Global Health, 2018
95% of these children (50.2M) live in low- and middle-income countries. Lancet Global Health, 2018
16.3% of Nairobi children (ages 0–3) are developmentally off-track—3× the rate of international peers. Aga Khan University, 2025

But numbers only tell half the story.

Behind these numbers is a caregiver trying to understand why their child isn't speaking. Wondering whether they're doing enough. Feeling exhausted after constantly advocating, teaching, and caring for their child—while simply keeping their family afloat.

A child’s development doesn't happen only in clinics or classrooms—it happens at home during ordinary moments: playing, eating, and getting dressed. By equipping caregivers during these daily routines, we turn every day into an opportunity for growth.

What We Do

Bringing evidence-based support closer to families.

Illustrative image

We deliver the WHO Caregiver Skills Training (CST)—an internationally validated program tailored for communities with limited access to specialized care. Delivered by trained local facilitators, CST equips parents with practical strategies to turn daily home routines into opportunities for growth.

Core Focus Areas

Communication & Engagement

Simple techniques to help non-verbal or delayed children connect and communicate.

Daily Living Skills

Practical strategies for teaching independence in eating, dressing, and hygiene.

Behavior & Wellbeing

Positive management for challenging behaviors alongside tools to protect caregiver mental health.

Building for Scale

We do not just run a program—we build sustainable systems. By tracking implementation costs, measuring real-world outcomes, and adapting to local contexts, we generate the evidence needed to integrate caregiver support into Kenya’s broader healthcare and community networks.

How We Work

With our heads. With our hearts. With our whole selves.

Head:
Evidence

We follow the evidence.

We use approaches grounded in rigorous research and measure what happens in practice. We test assumptions, learn from implementation, and change course when the evidence changes.

Heart:
Family

We build with, not for.

Caregivers are experts in their own families. Practitioners, community organizations, and local leaders bring knowledge and relationships that cannot be replicated from the outside. We listen first and design alongside the people closest to the problem.

Soul:
Community

We strengthen what already exists.

We won't try to do everything ourselves. We work through trusted community networks, build local capacity, and partner where others are better positioned. Over time, we want effective approaches to become part of sustainable systems rather than parallel ones.

Where We Work

Starting in Nairobi. Building across Kenya.

Why Kenya?

Kenya is at an important moment for disability inclusion and early childhood development. A new Persons with Disabilities National Policy was adopted in 2024, followed by a new Persons with Disabilities Act in 2025 and a Joint Disability Inclusion Strategy for 2025–2027.

The policy direction is increasingly clear. The opportunity now is implementation.

Kenya also has a growing ecosystem of organizations, practitioners, researchers, advocates, and government partners already working across developmental disabilities, early childhood development, health, parenting, and social services.

We don't want to reinvent that ecosystem. We want to learn from it, contribute to it, and help make it stronger.

Why Nairobi?

We're starting in Nairobi because of its combination of need, infrastructure, and opportunity. Nairobi has a strong concentration of organizations, health and education institutions, researchers, practitioners, community organizations, and government stakeholders working across the issues that matter to families.

It gives us the opportunity to work closely with families and local partners, test what works in a real-world setting, and build the relationships and infrastructure needed for sustainable scale.

Nairobi is our starting point, not our endpoint. Our goal is to build an approach that can be sustainably delivered across Kenya—and to share what we learn with others working to expand evidence-based caregiver support around the world.

Nairobi is where we start. Kenya is where we aim to grow.
